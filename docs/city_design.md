# City laboratory, housing-0.5.0/city

The city generator starts with a coarse 4–5 by 4–5 block grid. A seeded grammar varies each block's width and height, draws connected arterial and local street edges, and reserves two parks and one water block. A pedestrian shortcut crosses each park. Remaining blocks receive nine required services and family homes. Each developed plot has a frontage path to its real building entrance. Public connectivity and every building interior are validated independently; the route endpoint joins the two graphs at an explicit entrance link.

The nine services are hospital, school, fire station, fitness centre, office hub, town hall, nightclub, shopping centre, and supermarket. Hospital, school, and fire station use the existing civic room programs. Other services deliberately reuse existing gym, party, office, dining, pantry, reception, library, and bathroom rooms as labelled prototype interiors. They do not claim bespoke shopping or council furniture. The catalog reports `program_fallback` per plot. `capacity.jobs` counts work furniture with a reachable anchor; `office_units` counts generated office rooms; `equipped_places` counts furniture with explicit sitting or exercise capacity. `area_based_occupant_estimate` is marked as an estimate and is separate from furnished places. Classroom student counts come from the actual chairs.

The separate `/city` lab shows zones, street hierarchy, plots, building plans, and a public-to-room route. It has deterministic seed, block count, pan and zoom controls. It does not write to the main world or imported map data.

The main map has an append-only service extension south of y=153: fitness, nightlife, town hall, hospital, fire station, office, supermarket, and shopping centre. Original object IDs 1–349 and old terrain cells are unchanged. Eight new station kinds have valid anchors and connected paths from the existing neighborhood. This main-world extension is a fixed demonstration, whereas the city lab is a separately generated plan; they are not automatically synchronized.

Current limits: orthogonal blocks, fixed service mix, no terrain slope, zoning policy, traffic simulation, realistic building envelope optimizer, or automatic transfer of an experimental city plan into the running world. The regional service programs are prototypes; further purpose-built rooms and lot grammars can replace them without changing the route contract.
