# Living Neighborhood: active new-save map

`LivingNeighborhood(seed)` is a separate, deterministic `SpatialService` layout for newly selected `neighborhood-v1` saves. It does not rewrite the legacy Mosswood map or claim that the city-planning laboratory is already a fully navigable multi-floor world. The active map is a 128 × 180 metre ground plane; all house, public-service, work and leisure stations on that plane have checked interaction anchors.

## From streets to rooms

Two arterial cross streets, local lanes, edge connectors, and four staggered mid-block walks form the public skeleton. Twenty homes occupy smaller lots on four quieter lanes. The school, clinic, fire station, studio, workshop, shops, workplaces, gym and nightclub sit around two central greens rather than appearing in a distant service strip. Blocks and house frontages vary by seed; all building doors have a paved link to a street. A global flood check rejects any disconnected frontage, entrance or object anchor. This verifies reachability, though it is not traffic simulation.

The twenty `home_01`…`home_20` buildings keep `household_01`…`household_20` and support the existing 44-actor opening population. Three house plans differ in width and partition outline. Each has a 12 m² kitchen, separate living and dining zones, a private sleeping area, and a 6 m² bathroom. Three-person households have a walled child bedroom with a door. Some couples have a two-place double bed; others have two singles. Work surfaces, fridge, sink, family table, toilet and shower retain the existing action kinds; each household has exactly enough physical bed capacity and dining anchors. `spawn_for(household_id, member_index)` returns distinct, walkable interior ground-floor cells. The entry includes a `plan_variant` and six or seven named room regions.

The school has 24 individual pupil desks and chairs in four rows, an actual chalkboard, and a teacher work station with six physical anchors. Hospital treatment beds, studio and workshop departments, office workstations, shop/café counters and all eight new civic/leisure stations are actual objects. Every declared capacity is at most the number of its walkable anchors; capacities are not extrapolated from floor area. The service building IDs and station kinds retain the existing action/work assignment contract. There are no fictional upstairs jobs or rooms on this active map. `park_patrol` is a contiguous, walkable loop inside Clover Green.

`planning_metadata` is included in `public_data()` and new-save metadata. Its `layout_id` is `neighborhood-v1`; roads and parks use `[x, y, width, height]` metre-cell bounds, road classes are `arterial`, `local` and `pedestrian`, and `runtime_floors: [0]` makes the 2-D scope explicit. The generated `validated_entrances`, `validated_object_anchors` and `connected_walkable_cells` are runtime geometry checks, not architectural or accessibility certification.

## Verification and limits

`python -m unittest tests.test_neighborhood -v` checks household identities and furnishings, unequal room areas, every declared station and seat, capacity/anchor consistency, occupied-cell overlap, distinct interior spawns, deterministic replay and eight seed layouts. The geometry is a hand-authored block grammar with deterministic variation, not OpenStreetMap or a parcel/traffic solver. Gardens and ordinary grass are traversable under the legacy spatial contract; paved frontage and street metadata distinguish intended routes, while weighted route preference is a separate navigation concern.
